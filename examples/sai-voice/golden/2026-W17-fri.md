Anthropic's Claude Mythos launch got the safety logic backwards

Most people read Project Glasswing as responsible stewardship. Restrict a powerful cybersecurity model to 40 organizations, keep it out of bad hands, protect the internet. That framing is everywhere right now.

What it misses: attackers do not apply to waitlists. They share tools in private channels, iterate fast, and do not wait for a press release. Defenders, on the other hand, are exactly the kind of institutions that go through formal access programs. So a 40-org cap on Claude Mythos does not symmetrically slow both sides. It slows the side that follows rules.

OpenAI read this differently. GPT-5.4-Cyber went to thousands of defenders. Broad distribution to the people actively patching networks, triaging alerts, and writing detection rules. I watched security teams I know go from months-long vulnerability reviews to findings in seconds after getting access. That asymmetry matters more than the headline restriction numbers.

To be fair to Anthropic, I think the Glasswing model reflects a genuine belief that concentrated, auditable deployment produces better feedback and fewer catastrophic misuses early on. With Anthropic capturing 37 percent of enterprise AI spending right now, they have real use to set norms, and they are betting that quality of deployment beats quantity.

But I think they are solving for the wrong tail risk. The catastrophic scenario is not a vetted defender misusing Mythos. It is defenders being outpaced because access was rationed.

Do you think restricted rollouts protect more than they cost, or is broad defender access the only move that actually shifts the odds?
