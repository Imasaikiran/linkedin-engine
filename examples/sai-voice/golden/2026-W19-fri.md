Anthropic and OpenAI's forward-deployed engineer bets may be a trap

The common read is that embedding engineers directly into mid-market companies is how frontier labs finally crack enterprise adoption at scale.

What that read misses: Anthropic's $1.5B joint venture with Blackstone, Goldman Sachs, and Hellman & Friedman, and OpenAI's parallel $4B venture with TPG, Bain, and Brookfield, both depend on a labor model that does not compound. You are not building a product flywheel. You are staffing a consulting practice with a Claude logo on it.

Forward-deployed engineers generate revenue and generate insight. But the insight stays trapped in bespoke workflows inside portfolio companies. It rarely feeds back into the model or the platform in ways that widen the moat. What you get is a high-touch, high-cost engagement that looks like SaaS ARR on a pitch deck but behaves like professional services on a P&L.

I watched a mid-market rollout take 14 months to reach steady state because every edge case required an engineer on a call. That is not a distribution strategy. That is an account management problem with better branding.

The charitable version of this bet: both labs are buying ground truth data on how real companies actually work, and that corpus will eventually train something worth the price. That would make the services layer a research investment, not a revenue model.

I am genuinely uncertain which version is true. Are you building a moat or renting one?
